The robot is a simple crawler consisting of two \hyperlink{kos}{Kresling origami springs (KOS)} that are mirror images of each other, connected in series, with the \hyperlink{actuation}{actuation unit} in between.
Both springs can expand and contract in sync, producing a crawling-like motion when combined with specially designed ``feet''.
For the purpose of demonstrating target adaptation, however, the movement of the robot as a whole is not very interesting.
Rather, the state of the KOSs is the state that will be controlled.
Since, a KOS can be actuated both by linear motion (pushing or pulling) and by rotational motion (twisting), and the two motions are directly related (\cref{subsec:kresling-origami})\todo{\cref{subsec:kresling-origami}}, a contracting or expanding motion can be measured as the angle of the KOS' twist.
